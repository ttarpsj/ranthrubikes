How to Dress for Winter Cycling: A Temperature Guide

Winter riding presents a different challenge every day. A cold morning can become a mild afternoon, while wind, rain and humidity can make the same temperature feel dramatically different.

Dressing appropriately is therefore less about wearing as many layers as possible and more about balancing insulation, ventilation and freedom of movement.

This guide divides winter riding into four practical temperature ranges, with suggested clothing combinations for each.

Temperature ranges are approximate. Wind, humidity, effort intensity, individual tolerance and ride duration all affect the clothing required.

Lightness and Protection for Changing Temperatures

Adaptable layers for cool mornings and milder afternoons.

At these temperatures, the challenge is managing changing conditions without overheating. Lightweight jerseys, breathable bib shorts and a thin base layer provide a comfortable starting point.

A packable vest adds protection during descents or wind-exposed sections and can be removed when the temperature rises.

More Insulation, Without Sacrificing Ventilation

Additional protection as temperatures begin to fall.

As temperatures fall, long-sleeve jerseys and insulating layers become increasingly useful. The goal is to retain warmth at lower intensities while allowing excess heat to escape during harder efforts.

PACER SOLID, HYDER and WEEZER offer different levels of insulation. Wind-resistant vests such as PLUS and VECTOR add flexibility, while the SELKIE 2.0 jacket provides another option for variable weather.

More Protection Against Cold and Changing Intensity

Wind protection and insulation for colder rides.

At lower temperatures, wind chill and moisture become more important. Climbing can generate significant heat, while descending exposes the body to rapid cooling.

Insulated jackets such as ELEMENT, VANTAGE and ENVY 3.0 provide different levels of protection. Thermal bib tights and an appropriate base layer help maintain warmth without restricting movement.

Maximum Protection When the Cold Bites

Thermal layering for demanding winter conditions.

When temperatures approach freezing or fall below it, each layer needs a specific purpose. Outerwear should reduce exposure to wind and moisture, insulation should retain warmth, and base layers should help manage perspiration.

SKIMO PRO, ARMOUR and SUPERARMOUR are options for demanding conditions. SUBZERO thermal tights provide additional wind protection, while insulated gloves, merino socks and overshoes protect exposed extremities.

The objective is a balanced system rather than excessive layering, since trapped perspiration can become a source of discomfort during long descents.

One Winter, Different Ways to Prepare

The same temperature can require very different clothing depending on the route, weather and intensity of the ride.

Use temperature as a starting point, then adjust your layers to the conditions. A packable vest may be sufficient on a mild morning, while longer winter rides demand greater protection for the body and extremities.

The right clothing should allow you to focus on the ride rather than the weather.
