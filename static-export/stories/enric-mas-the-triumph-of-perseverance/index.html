PRO CYCLING · September 15, 2026

Enric Mas: The Triumph of Perseverance

Enric Mas first stood on the podium of La Vuelta a España in 2018. Just 23 years old, he finished second in Madrid and established himself as one of Spanish cycling’s most promising Grand Tour contenders. In the years that followed, he remained a consistent presence near the top of the general classification, but an overall victory continued to elude him.

In Granada, that changed. At 31, the Movistar Team rider claimed his first Grand Tour victory, winning La Vuelta a España 2026 after three demanding weeks of racing against some of the strongest riders in the peloton.

For Mas, the result represented the culmination of years spent pursuing the same objective. His career had become closely associated with La Vuelta, a race in which he repeatedly demonstrated the ability to compete with the world’s best without quite reaching the top step.

The 2026 edition initially appeared to offer another formidable challenge. Tadej Pogačar entered among the leading favorites and made his intentions clear from the opening stages, attacking aggressively whenever the terrain offered an opportunity.

Stage six, between Alcossebre and Castelló, provided an early indication that Mas might be capable of changing the narrative. On the climb to El Bartolo, Pogačar accelerated away from the favorites. Mas responded, closed the gap, and reached the summit alongside him.

Although Pogačar won the stage in the sprint, Mas had demonstrated that he could match one of cycling’s most explosive riders on decisive terrain. The performance suggested that Movistar’s ambitions could extend beyond another podium finish.

Two days later, the race changed dramatically when Pogačar crashed and abandoned. Mas inherited the race lead and the red jersey, but the greatest challenge was still ahead: defending that advantage through nearly two weeks of mountainous racing.

He responded immediately, winning the following stage to Alto de Aitana and strengthening his position in the general classification. From that point forward, his race became one of discipline, consistency, and calculated defense.

As the final week unfolded, Primož Roglič, Felix Gall, and the remaining contenders attempted to reduce the deficit. Mas answered each significant challenge, relying on his climbing consistency and the collective strength of Movistar Team.

Stage 20 offered the final major examination. With nearly 5,000 meters of accumulated climbing, the route demanded both endurance and tactical control. Mas not only protected his lead but extended his advantage over Roglič to two minutes and fifteen seconds.

A Victory Years in the Making

By the final stage in Granada, the overall result was effectively settled. Mas crossed the finish line as the winner of La Vuelta a España, securing the first Grand Tour title of his career.

The victory carried significance beyond the result itself. Years of near misses, repeated podium finishes, and sustained performances had finally produced the outcome he had pursued since emerging as a young contender in 2018.

It was also a collective achievement. Movistar Team spent the race controlling the peloton, protecting its leader, and responding to the challenges of rival teams. Their work transformed an individual opportunity into a defining result.

For Mas, the red jersey was more than a symbol of three successful weeks. It represented the persistence required to remain competitive at the highest level, season after season, until opportunity and preparation finally aligned.
